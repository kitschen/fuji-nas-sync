Fuji NAS Sync

Welcome to Fuji NAS Sync

This app receives the photos from your Fujifilm camera's PC AUTO SAVE function and writes them to your NAS, in place of Fujifilm's Windows / Mac software. The camera and the Raspberry Pi meet on your home network: you start the transfer on the camera, the Pi copies every photo it hasn't copied before.

        Destination name
        The name the camera lists when
      registering and shows when it starts PC AUTO SAVE, like a computer name. Letters, digits and dashes, no spaces.
      Cameras must register again after a change. If you replace a Windows PC, you can use that
      PC's name here (turn the PC's PC AutoSave off) and the camera does not need to register again.
        
    
        Quick scan
        The camera offers every file on the card
      and reading the list takes a quarter of a second per file: about 17 minutes for 4000 photos. A quick scan reads
      from the newest file backwards and stops at files that are already copied, usually in a few seconds.
        
      
        Photos shot after deleting others in the camera can land in the gaps instead of at the end. The app notices
      that from the number of files and the card's free space and then reads the whole card. Click Full scan
      in the Cameras tab to force it once.
        
    
        Network interface
        Before each transfer the app checks
      that the NAS is reached through this interface, usually eth0 (the network cable). Leave empty if
      the Pi uses Wi-Fi.
        
  
        

  
        
    
        
      
        How it works
        
      
        The camera joins your home Wi-Fi and looks for its registered destination by name. The Pi answers, the
        camera connects to it and the Pi copies all photos and movies it has not copied before. The camera shows the
        progress (for example 3/12) and turns itself off when it is done. Nothing is deleted from the
        card.
        
      
          
        
        • The camera and the Pi must be in the same network (subnet), because the camera finds the Pi with a
          broadcast. A Pi on a network cable at the same router is fine.
          • 
        
        • Older cameras like the X-E2 only use 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi.
          • 
        
        • No firewall on the Pi may block UDP and TCP ports 51541 and 51542.
          • 
        
        • On a computer that also runs Fujifilm's PC AutoSave, only one of the two can listen. Turn Fujifilm's
          off (Windows service FFPCAutoSave).
          •

        The camera does not remember what it sent, so it offers every file on the card each time. The Pi skips the
        files it has copied before (same camera, file name, capture time and size). Reading a file's details takes
        about a quarter of a second, so the Pi reads from the newest file backwards and stops at the ones it already
        has (quick scan). It reads the whole card on the first transfer, when photos were deleted in the
        camera, every 30 days, and when you click Full scan: with a full card that takes some minutes.
        
      
        If a transfer is interrupted, the next one continues, including the file that was in progress.
